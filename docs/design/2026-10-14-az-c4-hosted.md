# az-c4: the closed-list C4 network as a hosted bot that may play humans

Status: hosted, opt-in, experimental (adoption rung 2 of the README's ladder).
Not promoted, not a default, and not measured against `bot` on the held-out
gate. Unlike az-c3 it is offered against humans.

## What it is

`az-c4` is the az search seat with the C4 network of the closed-list,
census-on aztrain loop (`/mnt/sata/gorge-training/aztrain/PLAN.md`, section
"Census on, closed opponent list (2026-10-14)"). It is the policy that loop
evaluated:

```
az:sims=100:autopay:oppnodes:oppfull:morekinds:closedlist:topk=6:gonet=c4.dzgw
```

plus the London mulligan by land count (`dzgorge play -mull-heuristic`), with
the engine's paid-cost mana census at its default (on), as in training.

**Closed list** is the difference from az-c3. The seat knows its own deck
list and never the opponent's. At every searched decision the opponent's
unseen hand, library and sideboard are re-identified from a belief: a list
drawn uniformly from the 28,366 TRAIN-split 17lands FDN decks whose colour
identity covers the colours of the opponent's seen cards, minus what the
seat has seen (`searchprobe.Belief`, `azmcts.WorldRedealClosed`).

Code: `bots/azc4` is the entry. It shares az-c3's implementation:
`bots/azc3.Hosted` is a hosted az policy parameterised by its `bots.Info` and
whether it is closed-list only, and az-c3 is now one instance of it (its
behaviour, errors and flags are unchanged). `azc4.Config(b)` is
`azc3.ClosedConfig(b)`; `TestConfigIsTheClosedMeasuredSpec` pins it.
`azc4.Enable(net, belief)` refuses a nil belief, and the factory refuses to
build a seat without one.

## What was measured

Source: `/mnt/sata/gorge-training/aztrain/cloop-census/eval-c4-*.jsonl.summary.json`,
the loop's own evaluation. Each row is 600 paired games (300 deck pairs,
seats swapped), 100 simulations, closed list, census on, on the FDN **eval**
decks, `-mulligans 2 -mull-heuristic`, seed 78. Intervals are Wilson 95%.

| C4 against | wins - losses | score | 95% interval | ms per searched decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sb-tactical | 407 - 193 | 67.8% | [64.0, 71.4] | 134 |
| the same closed search, no net | 365 - 235 | 60.8% | [56.9, 64.7] | 110 |
| C3 of the same loop | 322 - 278 | 53.7% | [49.7, 57.6] | 134 |
| best census-off closed net (cloop-closed c3) | 331 - 269 | 55.2% | [51.2, 59.1] | 141 |

For comparison, the open-list c3 (az-c3's Info) measured 63.0% [59.1, 66.8]
vs sb-tactical. The C4 measurements were taken with the closed list, so none
of them used the opponent's real list. One caveat from the audit below: the
bench, like the host before this change, searched decisions posed inside a
resolution from roots whose re-run checkpoint holds the real hidden cards
(2 of 271 searched decisions in the privacy test's games). The hosted
seat no longer searches those decisions at a table with a human.

## Why it may play humans: the gating design

Operator ruling 2026-10-13: an opponent's decklist is private. Before this
change the host refused every `bots.Info.Env` policy at a table with a human.

- `bots.Info.ClosedLists` (new) declares an Env policy whose every seat closes
  its opponents' lists. `bots.ReadsDecklists` / `Info.ReadsDecklists()` is now
  `Env && !ClosedLists`, and it is the one predicate every gate uses: host
  table validation (`server/host/table.go`, `server/host/seatpolicy.go`), gorged's offered
  set and default (`cmd/gorged/botpolicies.go`), the vs-bot create flow
  (`cmd/gorged/game.go`), draft events (`cmd/gorged/events.go`), draftsim and
  draftlab. az-c3, az-redeal, sb-tactical and every other open-list Env policy
  are refused at human tables exactly as before.
- The declaration is not trusted (`server/host/closedlist.go`). At a table with a
  human the host checks the seats it actually built, after the human slots are
  filled and before the first decision: every non-human `bots.EnvSeat` must be
  a `searchseat.ClosedListSeat` with a non-nil `SearchBelief`, or the match
  crashes with nothing handed out (`closedListGuard`). At every decision
  `envData` re-checks: an open Setup is cut to the actor's own list and gets no
  root (`closedSetup`), and no root is built at a decision posed inside a
  resolution (`closedRootRefusal`, below). Nothing a client sends can change
  either: the policy is resolved server-side and the seats are built by the
  host.
- gorged: `-az-c4-net`, `-az-c4-decks`, `-az-c4-belief` (required),
  `-az-c4-belief-pool`, `-az-c4-demo-table` (default on). No
  `-allow-decklist-bots`. The policy is offered in the bot picker; its pool is
  reserved for it like az-c3's (both seats dealt from it). `-az-c4-decks` may be
  the same directory as `-az-c3-decks`: a shared deck is reserved for both,
  catalogued once (`DeckInfo.Pool` = `az-c3,az-c4`, and `?policy=` lists it for
  either), and a request that names no policy but picks a shared deck plays
  az-c4 (az-c3 cannot seat a human). Two different directories holding the same
  id are refused. Draft events never offer a policy with a reserved pool.

## Leak audit

Every path by which the az-c4 seat or its search could read the opponent's
real hidden information, in closed mode at a human table:

| Path | Finding | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| `Setup.Decks` | Opponent's list dropped (`PublicGame.Closed`). | none needed |
| `Setup.Names` | Deck names; the protocol already shows them to every seat (`SeatInfo.Deck`). | none; public by the protocol |
| `Setup.Tokens` | The server-wide token corpus, not deck-derived. | none needed |
| Root hand/library identities | Re-identified from the belief (existing closed redeal). | none needed |
| Root **sideboard** objects | LEAK: the closed redeal touched only hand and library; the opponent's sideboard objects kept their real cards. | `Redealer.reidentifySide` re-identifies them from the belief, after every other deal (the hand/library deal and its RNG stream are unchanged); `rules.ReidentifyHidden` and the `HiddenIdentity` fold accept a sideboard card. FDN decks have no sideboards, so the served game is unchanged. |
| Root `deckManifests` (`OwnDeck(opponent)`) | LEAK: every clone shared the real manifest table, so `OwnDeck(opp)` named the opponent's list and sideboard. Opponent-perspective simulations filled `Board.OwnDeck`/`OwnLibrary` from it (no decision code read those fields for C4's entity features, so no answer changed). | `rules.Engine.ClosedDeckManifests` / `UseDeckManifests`: every closed world carries only the seat's own manifest; a posed-resolution re-run keeps it (`resolveBoard.Restore`). |
| Root offer-walk class cache (`walkObjCls`) | LEAK (latent): the stale per-object fingerprint held each re-identified object's REAL card until the next catch-up. | `ReidentifyHidden` drops the re-identified object's cached class (`forgetWalkClass`); it was refreshed from the log before any read anyway. |
| Root tape checkpoint `S0` at a decision posed inside a resolution | LEAK: every clone of a posed engine shares the resolution's checkpoint, the live game as the resolution began with every hidden card real, and re-runs from it when answered. | At a table with a human no root is built there (`posedRefusal`); the seat plays its non-searched fallback. 2 of 271 searched decisions in the privacy test. Bot-only tables and the bench are unchanged. |
| Feed collector | LEAK: `noPot.Chars` kept a pointer to the live engine after each capture. | Cleared when the capture returns (`Collector.observe`). |
| `Board` (`env.Board`, `Search.Board`, the non-searched Board path) | LEAK: the host's reused Board carried its incremental row cache, which holds the live `state.Game` and log. | The host hands seats `Board.Detached()` (`bots/botpolicy/board_detach.go`); every Board-based hosted bot gets it. |
| Redealer pool check | Skipped for a closed player: nothing of the real list is compared or kept. For the seat's own (open) list it compares the seat's own hidden cards. | none needed |
| Redealer projection check (`KnownCards.holds`) | LEAK (refusal text): on a mismatch the reason named the engine's card, and the reason reaches the seat as `env.RootRefused`. Fires only when the seat's own accounting is wrong. | The reason names only what the seat believes. The refusal itself remains a one-bit signal that depends on hidden state; it does not fire in normal play. |
| `compiledText` (engine card-text cache) | Holds every configured deck's cards (`pin`) and tables keyed by their faces. Unexported, shared by every clone, and every lookup is a pure function of the card queried, so it can change speed, never an answer. | Not fixed (rebuilding it per root costs a compile per decision); the only field the privacy test does not walk. |
| Pool gates (`setNameInPool`, `layer4InPool`, `controlStaticInPool`) | Three genesis bools over both lists; they gate refresh work, not results. | Not fixed; residual. |
| Object ids | Genesis assigns ids in list order, so an opponent id adjacent to a revealed card's id is likely a copy of it. The closed redeal deals unknown objects in id order with shuffled belief cards, so a world's id holds nothing; but an engine that reasoned over raw ids could infer list grouping. Nothing in the search does. | Not fixed; residual. |
| `View` | The seat's own projection, the same one a human gets. | none needed |

Separately, the smoke found a hosted bug that had nothing to do with privacy
but made the hosted search seats stop searching: when an az seat answered with
a payment plan, the copy of the decision the host recorded on the seat's feed
had no `PaymentActions` (the engine builds them lazily), the answer did not
translate, and the feed stopped for the rest of the game, so every later
decision was refused a root and played the fallback. A first smoke game had
109 refused roots out of 109. `server/host/match.go` now builds the plans before
copying the decision when the answer carries one. After the fix: 1 refused
root in 15 smoke games (the sidecar keeps the count, not the reason; the
posed-resolution refusal is the expected one). This affected az-c3 and
az-redeal on hosted tables too.

## What the privacy test proves

`server/host/hosted_azc4_test.go` `TestAZC4NeverSeesTheHumansHiddenCards` plays three
whole games through the real host (`Registry`, a table with `Humans`, no
opt-in): the real az-c4 seat (synthetic network, a five-deck Foundations
belief) against a human seat answered by the host's own timeout caretaker.
The human's deck is mono-red goblins with the mono-black aggro list as its
sideboard; the 16 names in neither the belief nor the bot's deck are the
secret cards.

Ground truth comes from the live game, independently of the feed and the
redealer: a secret card counts as revealed once its object has been the
subject of an event into a public zone or onto the stack, named in a public
reveal, or shown in a View handed to the bot. At every decision the bot is
asked (278: 271 searched, 7 on the Board path), the test walks, by reflection
through unexported fields, everything the host handed it: the Env's Setup,
the honest root engine (G, log, caches, tape checkpoint, manifests, every
field but `compiledText`), the Board, the feed (its collector, history and
identities) and the refusal reason. A secret name (a string containing it, or
a pointer to the card, its faces or their abilities, triggers, statics or
replacements) found there and not yet revealed fails the test. It also
requires a root at every searched decision but the posed-resolution ones.

Each fix above was found by this test failing first (sideboard, manifests,
walk-class cache, Board cache, feed collector, posed checkpoint) and it fails
again if any is reverted; the payment-record fix is pinned by the root count
(reverting it gives 73 "no live observation feed" refusals).

What it covers: everything the host hands the seat, at the decision it is
handed, which is everything the search starts from (each search world is a
clone of the root plus a closed redeal of it). What it does not cover: the
`compiledText` cache (above), boolean pool gates (a walk cannot attribute a
bit), state the search builds after the hand-off (worlds are derived from the
root and the belief only), side channels (timing, a refusal's occurrence),
face-down or other identity-hidden public objects (none in these decks), and
decks other than the fixture's. `TestClosedListGuardRefusesAnOpenSeatAtAHumanTable`
shows the start check refuses an open-list Env seat swapped in behind an
az-c4 table before it is asked anything; `TestDecklistGateAtHumanTables`
shows az-c3, az-redeal and sb-tactical are still refused at a human table with
or without the opt-in, and az-c4 is accepted without it;
`TestHostedEnvSeatsIgnoreTheRealHiddenCardsAZC4` is the standard hidden-card
swap row.

## Hosted smoke

Built `gorged` from this branch, `127.0.0.1:8090`, persistence under
`/tmp/gorge-azc4-*`, no `-allow-decklist-bots`:

```
gorged -addr 127.0.0.1:8090 -tables 0 -vsbot -az-c4-demo-table=false \
  -dir /tmp/gorge-azc4-smoke-final-<pid>/data -feedback ... -art-dir ... \
  -az-c4-net /mnt/sata/gorge-training/az-c4/c4.dzgw \
  -az-c4-decks /mnt/sata/gorge-training/az-c3/decks \
  -az-c4-belief /mnt/sata/gorge-training/dzgorge-repro/inputs/decks \
  -az-c4-belief-pool /mnt/sata/gorge-training/dzgorge-repro/inputs/pool.tsv
```

- The server loads the belief (the pool's train split) at startup and was
  listening within 15 s. `/api/bot-policies` offers az-c4 (constructed, experimental); `/api/decks`
  is the 30 ordinary decks plus the 36 pooled, `?policy=az-c4` the 36.
- `POST /api/games {"bot_policy":"az-c4"}` creates the human table; 15 games
  played to a finish through the HTTP API as the human, passing priority and
  keeping hands (`pending` / `intent` with the seat token). None crashed or
  stalled; az-c4 won all 15; 1 refused root in the 15 after the
  payment-record fix.
- Latency, the wall time from the human's accepted intent to the human's next
  pending decision, polled every 3 ms, one game at a time on an otherwise idle
  server. 5 games: 697 waits included a bot decision, mean 12 ms, p50 4, p95
  92, max 242. The 73 waits of 20 ms or more (the ones carrying a bot search)
  mean **91 ms, p95 136**, max 242. A second 5-game batch: 522 waits, mean 15,
  p95 88, max 187. The az-c4 Info quotes the 73-wait figures.
- A second server with az-c3 too (`-az-c3-net`, `-allow-decklist-bots`, both
  policies on the one deck directory): `/api/bot-policies` offers az-c4 and not
  az-c3; `POST /api/games {"bot_policy":"az-c3"}` is refused (400, not
  offered); a request naming only a pooled human deck plays az-c4; both demo
  tables (az-c3 vs az-c3, az-c4 vs az-c4) run; `/api/decks` lists the 36
  shared decks once, tagged `az-c3,az-c4`.

Servers were stopped by the pid listening on 8090.

## How to serve it

```
gorged -vsbot \
  -az-c4-net /mnt/sata/gorge-training/az-c4/c4.dzgw \
  -az-c4-decks /mnt/sata/gorge-training/az-c3/decks \
  -az-c4-belief /mnt/sata/gorge-training/dzgorge-repro/inputs/decks \
  -az-c4-belief-pool /mnt/sata/gorge-training/dzgorge-repro/inputs/pool.tsv
```

`-az-c4-demo-table=false` drops its bot-only spectator table. With az-c3 on
the same server, pass the same directory to `-az-c3-decks`.

Demo deploy (the operator's to run):

```
AZ_C4_NET=/mnt/sata/gorge-training/az-c4/c4.dzgw \
AZ_C4_DECKS=/mnt/sata/gorge-training/az-c3/decks \
AZ_C4_BELIEF=/mnt/sata/gorge-training/dzgorge-repro/inputs/decks \
AZ_C4_BELIEF_POOL=/mnt/sata/gorge-training/dzgorge-repro/inputs/pool.tsv \
  make deploy-demo
```

`ops/scripts/deploy-demo.sh` validates the three required variables together
(and the pool file when set), passes them without `-allow-decklist-bots`, and
fills the card-art cache for `AZ_C4_DECKS` when it differs from `AZ_C3_DECKS`.
The AZ_C3 variables can be set alongside.

The net and the decks are not in the repository (training output and
17lands data, CC BY 4.0).

## What was not measured

- **Against `bot` on the held-out gate**, and **against a human who plays**:
  every strength number is bot against bot; the smoke's human only passes.
- **Hosted latency under load**: the smoke ran one game at a time with no
  demo table.
- **The cost of the posed-resolution fallback** on strength: 2 of 271
  searched decisions in the privacy test played the non-searched answer.
