# az-c5: the C5 network, trained after the payment-plan fixes, as a hosted bot

Status: hosted, opt-in, experimental, beside az-c4. Not promoted and not a
default. Like az-c4 it is offered against humans.

## What it is

`az-c5` is az-c4 with a different network. The C5 net came from az-c4's
closed-list, census-on aztrain loop (`aztrain/cloop-census.sh`), continued for
four generations on a build that carries the payment-plan fixes. Everything
else is az-c4's:

- the search config:

  ```
  az:sims=100:autopay:oppnodes:oppfull:morekinds:closedlist:topk=6:gonet=c5.dzgw
  ```

- the London mulligan by land count;
- the paid-cost mana census (on);
- the belief (the TRAIN split of the 17lands FDN pool);
- the decks it may play;
- the closed-list contract.

The architecture is az-c4's too: `mlp` with 601,346 parameters.

Code:

- `bots/azc5` is the entry. Like `bots/azc4`, it is one `bots/azc3.Hosted`
  with its own `bots.Info`. `azc5.Config(b)` equals `azc4.Config(b)`, and
  `TestConfigIsTheClosedMeasuredSpec` pins that.
- gorged's az-c4 hosting code (`cmd/gorged/azclosed.go`, which was `azc4.go`)
  now takes the policy as a parameter. Each closed-list policy is a `closedAZ`
  with its own flags. az-c4's flags, errors, demo table, catalogue and loader
  are unchanged, and az-c5 is a second instance.
- The C5 weights are not in the repository.

## Recipe

|---|---|
| Loop | az-c4's closed-list census-on cloop (`aztrain/cloop-census.sh`), as `/mnt/sata/gorge-training/az-c5b/cloop.sh` |

| Gates | after each generation, 600 paired games against az-c4 and 600 against sb-tactical |
| C5 | generation 4: `g4.dzgw`, copied to `/mnt/sata/gorge-training/az-c5b/c5.dzgw` |

## What was measured

Each row is 600 paired games (300 deck pairs, seats swapped). Settings: FDN
**eval** split, seed 78, 100 simulations, closed list, census on,
`-mulligans 2 -mull-heuristic`. Every run had 0 errors, stalls and all-failed
searches. Intervals are Wilson 95%. Source:
`/mnt/sata/gorge-training/az-c5b/eval-*.jsonl.summary.json`.

| Net | vs az-c4 | vs sb-tactical |
|---|---|---|
| az-c4, on this build | n/a | 0.690 (414-186) [65.2, 72.6]; 0.663 before the payment fixes |
| gen 1 | 0.522 (313-287) | 0.712 (427-173) |
| gen 2 | 0.503 (302-298) | 0.710 (426-174) |
| gen 3 | 0.517 (310-290) | 0.705 (423-177) |
| **gen 4 = C5** | **0.528 (317-283) [48.8, 56.8]** | **0.723 (434-166) [68.6, 75.8]** |

What the table shows:

- C5 is the best of the four generations on both gates.
- Its edge over az-c4 is small: the head-to-head interval includes 50%.
- Against sb-tactical it is 3.3 pp above az-c4 on the same build. The two
  intervals overlap.
- Most of az-c4's gain on this build came from the payment fixes (0.663 to
  0.690), not from training.
- Searched decisions cost 114-117 ms on average at 24 workers, the same as
  az-c4 (114).

### Instant timing

The payment fixes made more instant-speed plays legal for the bots to find.
The probe asked whether the retrain then learned to use them. Method: the
`INSTANT_LOG` probe, 150 pairs against the bot, seed 4242. Data and
`analyze.py` are in `/mnt/sata/gorge-training/draftrl/instants/`.

| Instant casts | az-c4, before the fixes | az-c4, fixed | C5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| on the opponent's turn | 7.0% | 16.0% | 14.8% |
| in its own upkeep or draw step | 50.8% | 49.6% | 53.4% |
| while the opponent has a spell on the stack | 26% | 60% | 58% |

The payment fixes doubled reactive play: casts on the opponent's turn went
from 7.0% to 16.0%, and responses to a spell on the stack from 26% to 60%. The
retrain did not change timing. C5 is within about a point of fixed az-c4 on
every row. Self-play learns from visit counts, and those visit counts track
the prior, so the loop reinforces the timing the net already has. This
explanation was not tested separately.

## Ops lessons

- **`dzg.train --in-ram` over 27 GB of packs OOM-killed the box.** C5 streamed
  shards from disk instead. Training should stream unless the packs fit
  comfortably in memory.
- **`systemd-run --user` services default to `LimitNOFILE=1024`.** Training
  memmaps every shard of every pack. With 8 packs it ran out of file
  descriptors, and `cuInit` then could not open `/dev/nvidia*`. Torch reported
  this as "No CUDA GPUs are available", which looked like vLLM holding the
  cards. Generation 1 (7 packs) fit under the limit. The fix is
  `-p LimitNOFILE=524288` on the unit. The 5-try CUDA retry added while
  diagnosing this was kept and is harmless.

## Why it may play humans

Exactly as for az-c4 (`2026-10-14-az-c4-hosted.md`, "Why it may play humans"
and "Leak audit"):

- `bots.Info.ClosedLists` is set, so `bots.ReadsDecklists("az-c5")` is false.
- The host checks the seats it actually built (`server/host/closedlist.go`). It does
  not trust the declaration.
- Every decision's Setup and root are closed server-side.
- The belief comes from the server's `-az-c5-belief` flag only. Nothing a
  client sends selects or changes it.

Tests that cover az-c5:

- `server/host/hosted_azc5_test.go`:
  - `TestAZC5NeverSeesTheHumansHiddenCards` is az-c4's privacy proof for the
    az-c5 registration: three hosted games against a human, with a reflective
    leak scan of everything handed to the seat.
  - `TestHostedEnvSeatsIgnoreTheRealHiddenCardsAZC5` is the hidden-card swap
    row.
- `server/host/hosted_azc4_test.go`:
  - `TestDecklistGateAtHumanTables` accepts az-c5 at a human table without the
    opt-in.
  - `TestClosedListGuardRefusesAnOpenSeatAtAHumanTable/az-c5` crashes an az-c5
    table whose seat was swapped for an open-list one, before the seat is
    asked anything.
  - `TestHostedAZC4IsDeterministic` also plays an az-c5 table over the same
    synthetic network. It must reproduce az-c4's game intent for intent and
    head for head, which checks that az-c5 differs from az-c4 only in its
    network.
- `cmd/gorged/azclosed_test.go` runs the flag validation, flag registration,
  vs-human seating, demo table and shared-pool tests for both policies.
- `bots/azc5` mirrors `bots/azc4`'s tests: registration, refusals, config and
  factory.

## Hosted smoke

Built `gorged` from this branch and served it on `127.0.0.1:8090` with
persistence under `/tmp/gorge-azc5-smoke-*`. az-c4 and az-c5 shared one deck
directory, and az-c4's demo table was off:

```
gorged -addr 127.0.0.1:8090 -tables 0 -vsbot -prewarm=false -dir … -feedback … -art-dir … \
  -az-c4-net /mnt/sata/gorge-training/az-c4/c4.dzgw -az-c4-decks /mnt/sata/gorge-training/az-c3/decks \
  -az-c4-belief /mnt/sata/gorge-training/dzgorge-repro/inputs/decks \
  -az-c4-belief-pool /mnt/sata/gorge-training/dzgorge-repro/inputs/pool.tsv -az-c4-demo-table=false \
  -az-c5-net /mnt/sata/gorge-training/az-c5b/c5.dzgw -az-c5-decks /mnt/sata/gorge-training/az-c3/decks \
  -az-c5-belief /mnt/sata/gorge-training/dzgorge-repro/inputs/decks \
  -az-c5-belief-pool /mnt/sata/gorge-training/dzgorge-repro/inputs/pool.tsv
```

- **Policy list.** `/api/bot-policies` offers `az-c5`: experimental,
  constructed, labelled "az-c5 (C5 net, trained after the payment-plan fixes;
  closed decklists)". It is listed beside az-c4.
- **Deck catalogue.** `/api/decks` lists the 30 ordinary decks and the 36
  pooled decks. Each pooled deck is listed once with pool `az-c4,az-c5`, and
  `?policy=az-c5` returns the 36.
- **Bot-vs-bot table.** The `az-c5 vs az-c5 (FDN)` demo table (t1, bot-only, no
  `-allow-decklist-bots`) started with the server. It finished two matches
  (17 and 20 turns) while the smoke ran.
- **Games against a human.** `POST /api/games {"bot_policy":"az-c5"}` seated a
  human against az-c5. Six games were played to the end through the HTTP API
  as a human who passes priority and keeps every hand. None crashed or
  stalled, and az-c5 won all six. Both seats were dealt from the reserved
  pool.
- **Pick without a policy.** A request naming only a pooled human deck plays
  az-c4, the alphabetically first owner that may seat a human.
  `reservedPick` is unchanged.
- **az-c4 on the same server.** One az-c4 game played to the end.
- **Server log.** No errors, panics or refusals.
- **Latency.** Measured on the last 5 games, from the human's accepted intent
  to the human's next pending decision, polled every 3 ms. The games ran one
  at a time, with the az-c5 demo table playing on the same 4-CPU server.
  - All 619 waits that included a bot decision: mean 43 ms, p50 4, p95 131,
    max 266.
  - The 266 waits of 20 ms or more (the ones carrying a bot search): mean
    **97 ms, p95 173**. The az-c5 Info quotes these figures.
  - az-c4's smoke had no demo table competing for CPU and measured 91 ms and
    p95 136 for the same kind of waits.

The server was stopped by the pid listening on 8090.

## How to serve it

```
gorged -vsbot \
  -az-c5-net /mnt/sata/gorge-training/az-c5b/c5.dzgw \
  -az-c5-decks /mnt/sata/gorge-training/az-c3/decks \
  -az-c5-belief /mnt/sata/gorge-training/dzgorge-repro/inputs/decks \
  -az-c5-belief-pool /mnt/sata/gorge-training/dzgorge-repro/inputs/pool.tsv
```

- `-az-c5-demo-table=false` drops its bot-only spectator table.
- The az-c4 flags can be passed alongside with the same `-decks` directory.
  A shared deck is then reserved for both policies.

Demo deploy (the operator runs this):

```
AZ_C4_NET=/mnt/sata/gorge-training/az-c4/c4.dzgw \
AZ_C4_DECKS=/mnt/sata/gorge-training/az-c3/decks \
AZ_C4_BELIEF=/mnt/sata/gorge-training/dzgorge-repro/inputs/decks \
AZ_C4_BELIEF_POOL=/mnt/sata/gorge-training/dzgorge-repro/inputs/pool.tsv \
AZ_C5_NET=/mnt/sata/gorge-training/az-c5b/c5.dzgw \
AZ_C5_DECKS=/mnt/sata/gorge-training/az-c3/decks \
AZ_C5_BELIEF=/mnt/sata/gorge-training/dzgorge-repro/inputs/decks \
AZ_C5_BELIEF_POOL=/mnt/sata/gorge-training/dzgorge-repro/inputs/pool.tsv \
  make deploy-demo
```

What `ops/scripts/deploy-demo.sh` does with these variables:

- It validates each policy's three required variables together, and the pool
  file when it is set. The `closed_az` function does this for both policies.
- It passes the flags without `-allow-decklist-bots`.
- It fills the card-art cache for `AZ_C5_DECKS` only when that directory
  differs from both `AZ_C3_DECKS` and `AZ_C4_DECKS`.

With both policies set, the demo runs two bot-only search tables (az-c4 and
az-c5). The script has no variable for `-az-c4-demo-table` or
`-az-c5-demo-table`. Dropping a table means passing that flag to gorged by
hand.

## What was not measured

- **Against `bot` on the held-out gate, and against a human who plays.** Every
  strength number is bot against bot, and the smoke's human only passes.
- **Hosted latency under load.** The smoke ran one human game at a time.
- **A head-to-head result that separates C5 from az-c4.** If the 2.8 pp edge
  is real, about 1,200 paired games would put the 95% interval above 50%.
