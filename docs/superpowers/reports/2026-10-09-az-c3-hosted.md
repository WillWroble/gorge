# az-c3: the c3 self-play network as a hosted bot

Status: hosted, opt-in, experimental (adoption rung 2 of the README's ladder).
Not promoted, not a default, and not measured against `bot` on the held-out
gate.

## What it is

`az-c3` is the az search seat in its honest redeal world with the c3 network
from the aztrain self-play loop. It is the policy the loop evaluated:

```
az:sims=100:autopay:oppnodes:oppfull:morekinds:topk=6:gonet=c3.dzgw
```

plus the London mulligan by land count (`dzgorge play -mull-heuristic`). The
network is dzg's `mlp` (width 64, about 0.6M parameters, mostly the 16,384-row
hashed table), evaluated in process from a `.dzgw` file by `internal/gonet`.

The hosted entry is `bots/azc3`. `azc3.Config()` is the seat config; it is the
az-redeal hosted config (`azredeal.Hosted`: honest redeal world, no clairvoyant
source) with the keys above. `TestConfigMatchesTheMeasuredSpec` pins every
field.

## What was measured

Source: `/mnt/sata/gorge-training/aztrain/cloop/eval-c3-*.jsonl.summary.json`,
the loop's own evaluation (`cloop.sh`, 2026-10-08). Each row is 600 paired
games (300 deck pairs, seats swapped) at 100 simulations, on the FDN **eval**
decks (3,150 of the 31,516-deck pool, never trained on), `-mulligans 2
-mull-heuristic`, seed 78. Intervals are Wilson 95%.

| c3 against | wins - losses | score | 95% interval | ms per searched decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sb-tactical | 378 - 222 | 63.0% | [59.1, 66.8] | 314 |
| the same search, no net | 335 - 265 | 55.8% | [51.8, 59.8] | 202 |
| c2 | 307 - 293 | 51.2% | [47.2, 55.1] | 344 |

The earlier generations on the same decks, against sb-tactical: c1 58.5%
[54.5, 62.4], c2 61.7% [57.7, 65.5]. Most of the gain arrived with c1; c3 over
c2 is within noise.

The search runs honest worlds: `cmd/dzgorge/policy.go` sets
`cfg.World = azmcts.WorldRedeal`, so these numbers are not the clairvoyant
bench-only world's.

## What was not measured

- **Against `bot` on the held-out gate** (seed 1,000,000, 400 games per deck
  pair). That is the rung-3 bar; the loop evaluated against sb-tactical and
  the net-less search instead.
- **On any constructed deck.** The net's training decks are 17lands FDN limited
  decks. The demo's constructed and Commander decks are outside that
  distribution.
- **Hosted latency, beyond a smoke.** The 202-344 ms are the loop's, 24
  workers in parallel, through the bench driver. The hosted figure below is
  four games against a human who only passes: 32 waits that included bot
  search, mean 185 ms, p50 194, p95 259, max 277. Small, and not a loaded
  server.
- **Against a human.** Every number above is bot against bot.

## Hosting it

```
gorged -vsbot -az-c3-net /path/to/c3.dzgw -az-c3-decks /path/to/fdn-decks ...
```

`-az-c3-net` loads the network at startup (a bad file stops the server) and
registers the policy; without the flag the policy does not exist, so a server
that never asks for it cannot offer it. `-az-c3-decks` names the directory of
decks the policy may play and the only decks it may be played with; see the
deck pool below. The policy needs `-bot-auto-mana`, which is on by default.

The net is not in the repository (2.4 MB of training output). `GORGE_DZGW`
points the one test that reads a real network at it.

## Deck pool

The net saw only 17lands FDN limited decks, so the server keeps az-c3 there.
`-az-c3-decks DIR` names the decks it may play. They are loaded beside `-decks`
and reserved for the policy, both ways:

- a game against az-c3 deals from the pool for **both** seats (the human's deck
  shapes the states the net reads as much as its own does), and an explicit
  `human_deck` or `bot_deck` outside the pool is refused, naming the pool;
- a game against any other policy never deals a reserved deck, and an explicit
  pick of one is refused, naming the policy it belongs to;
- `GET /api/decks` lists every deck, the pool included (`DeckInfo.Pool` tags
  it); `?policy=az-c3` lists just the pool, and `?policy=<other>` the decks
  that are not reserved.

The web client never sends a bot policy, so a reserved deck chooses it: pick an
FDN deck as the human or the bot deck and the opponent is az-c3, with the other
seat dealt from the pool. Naming no decks keeps the server's default policy and
the ordinary decks. An FDN deck beside an ordinary one is refused, naming the
pool. No web change is needed: the picker lists what `/api/decks` returns.

The pool the 2026-10-09 smoke served is `/mnt/sata/gorge-training/az-c3/decks`:
the top three by rank in each colour pair with at least 300 training decks, 36
in all, from the train split only (the eval decks are in-distribution too, but
"trained on" is the claim the operator asked for). The files are 17lands data
(CC BY 4.0) and live outside the repository.

## Smoke

`gorged -vsbot -az-c3-net c3.dzgw -az-c3-decks <36 decks>` on 127.0.0.1:8090
(a throwaway directory, stopped afterwards): `/api/bot-policies` offers az-c3
(constructed, experimental); the default `/api/decks` is the 30 ordinary decks;
`?policy=az-c3` is the 36 pooled. Four games were played to a finish through
the HTTP API with the human seat passing priority; none crashed or stalled.

## Demo table

With `-az-c3-net`, the server adds one two-seat az-c3 vs az-c3 table
(`-az-c3-demo-table=false` to omit it), dealt from the pool and playing match
after match for spectators. It takes the next numbered id after `-tables`.

## Gauntlet

`botbench -spellbench az-c3,sb-tactical -spellbench-catalog fdn
-spellbench-decks-dir <pool> -spellbench-az-c3-net <net> -spellbench-mulligans 2`:
both policies play each deck as a mirror, seat-swapped, the SpellBench
methodology on the deployment pool. The run was planned at 25 pairs per deck
(1,800 games) and **stopped at 250 games**: az-c3 160-90, **64.0% [57.9, 69.7]**
against sb-tactical, across the pool's decks (interval Wilson 95%). The training
loop's 63.0% [59.1, 66.8] was on random pairs of eval decks, so the two agree on
different deck sets. Per-deck and per-colour results need the full run and are
not reported. The harness is `cmd/botbench/spellbench_c3.go`.

## Deploying

```
AZ_C3_NET=/mnt/sata/gorge-training/az-c3/c3.dzgw \
AZ_C3_DECKS=/mnt/sata/gorge-training/az-c3/decks \
  make deploy-demo
```

`scripts/deploy-demo.sh` passes both to the public (and omniscient) servers and
fills the card-art cache for the pool's cards first. Neither variable set is
the deploy it always was. The demo is the operator's to redeploy; this change
was staged on port 8091 only.
